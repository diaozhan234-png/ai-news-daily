Jensen Huang’s departure blasts big hole in AI startup funding, VCs and founders say
It was the resignation heard across the world. Jensen Huang, Nvidia’s embattled CEO who co-founded and led the company for 33 years, had officially stepped down . “It’s like a bomb went off,” one neocloud founder told TechCrunch when he first heard the news. Over the last several years, Nvidia has been a prolific investor , pouring tens of billions into startups annually and participating in 105 rounds this year alone, often as the largest investor, according to PitchBook. “If that direction changes, it’ll be a big blow to so many young companies,” said Maria Davidson, founder of Relational Capital. Huang’s absence leaves the startup ecosystem without one of its biggest champions and with far less capital for now, investors say, which could have a massive impact on dealmaking, valuations, funding, and even competition. “Nvidia is carrying almost every AI-native startup from application to neocloud,” Dawn Capital’s Shamillah Bankiya told TechCrunch. Most of the significant decisions to invest in startups often came from Jensen himself, according to Umesh Padval, managing partner at Seligman Ventures. Padval has known Huang since serving on the board of Mellanox Technologies, a networking chipmaker Nvidia acquired for approximately $7 billion in 2020. While there is hope that Huang’s eventual successor will resume his dealmaking spree, investors aren’t hopeful that it will happen in the short term. Nvidia’s interim CEO, Colette Kress, is expected to institute some changes , especially when it comes to Nvidia’s investments into AI startups at the crux of the billion-dollar data center build-out . Huang’s rampant dealmaking in this sphere has been a source of concern for Wall Street. “She’s much more conservative,” one investor said of Kress. “I don’t think she’s going to commit the same amount of money to keep the early-stage ecosystem going.” Nvidia’s biggest bets were backing neoclouds, inference providers, and open weight models, which are expensive for smaller companies to build. “Jensen’s initiatives are a big reason an ecosystem has emerged despite that,” Zain Javaid, co-founder of the hardware company Ghost, said. For instance, Nvidia backed CoreWeave when it was still private and participated in two of Crusoe’s rounds, including its $1.4 billion Series E. It bought Hugging Face this year for $13 billion (expected to close in 2027) and holds investments in Mistral AI, Lambda, and Black Forest Labs. Nvidia was often the largest investor in the deals. Last year, it wrote an $800 million check into a $2 billion Series B round for Reflection AI, before the open source startup began to earn meaningful revenue, according to the Wall Street Journal . “Jensen believed in open source and frankly speaking, Europe,” Bankiya said of how Huang invested so much in AI across the world. The cease of that support in other regions outside of America, like Europe “would be a massive funding and honestly ecosystem gap that no one can fill.” It’s also true that Nvidia was such a dominant player in the ecosystem that at times, it felt like a monopoly. By backing so many companies with such generous checks, Nvidia effectively locked those startups into using its chips, which helped reinforce its dominant market position. If the company does grow more conservative with its startup investments, that could leave more room for rivals and smaller competitors. (Coincidentally, Google announced two new chips this week amid Huang’s Nvidia fallout.) Some investors with whom TechCrunch spoke hope that large cloud companies and other chipmakers will step in to back startups and fill some of the void that will likely be left by Huang. Dharmesh Thakker, a general partner at Battery Ventures, for example, expects Big Tech rivals to vie for Nvidia’s investment crown, “depending upon who brings the best combination of a great product, an ecosystem and community to compete with CUDA, and the financial power to bankroll the ecosystem,” he said. CUDA is Nvidia’s software platform that enables developers to run AI models on its GPUs. While companies like Google, Amazon, Microsoft, and AMD may back mature startups to drive adoption of their chips and clouds, Thakker doubts they will pour hundreds of millions into pre-revenue infrastructure startups the way Nvidia did.

这是全世界听到的辞职。陷入困境的英伟达首席执行官黄仁勋（Jensen Huang，音译）正式卸任，他是英伟达33年的联合创始人和领导者。“这就像一枚炸弹爆炸了，”一位新云创始人在第一次听到这个消息时告诉TechCrunch。根据PitchBook的数据，在过去的几年里，英伟达一直是一个多产的投资者，每年向初创公司投入数百亿美元，仅今年就参与了105轮融资，通常是最大的投资者。Relational Capital的创始人玛丽亚·戴维森表示：“如果这个方向改变，对许多年轻公司来说将是一个巨大的打击。”。投资者表示，黄的缺席使创业生态系统失去了最大的冠军之一，目前资本也少得多，这可能会对交易、估值、融资甚至竞争产生巨大影响。Dawn Capital的Shamillah Bankiya告诉TechCrunch：“Nvidia几乎将所有AI原生初创公司从应用程序带到了新云。”。Seligman Ventures的管理合伙人Umesh Padval表示，投资初创公司的大多数重大决定往往来自Jensen本人。Padval自担任网络芯片制造商Nvidia于2020年以约70亿美元收购的Mellanox Technologies董事会成员以来就认识黄。虽然有希望黄的最终继任者将恢复他的交易狂潮，但投资者并不希望这会在短期内发生。Nvidia的临时首席执行官Colette Kress预计将做出一些改变，特别是在Nvidia对人工智能初创公司的投资方面，这是数十亿美元数据中心建设的关键。黄在这一领域的猖獗交易一直是华尔街关注的焦点。一位投资者在谈到克雷斯时说：“她要保守得多。”。“我认为她不会投入同样多的资金来维持早期生态系统的运转。”英伟达最大的赌注是支持新云、推理提供商和开放权重模型，这些模型对小公司来说建造起来很昂贵。硬件公司Ghost的联合创始人Zain Javaid表示：“尽管如此，Jensen的举措是生态系统出现的一个重要原因。”。例如，Nvidia在CoreWeave还是私有的时候就支持了它，并参与了Crusoe的两轮融资，包括其14亿美元的E轮融资。今年，它以130亿美元的价格收购了Hugging Face（预计将于2027年完成），并持有Mistral AI、Lambda和黑森林实验室的投资。英伟达通常是这些交易的最大投资者。据《华尔街日报》报道，去年，在开源初创公司Reflection AI开始获得有意义的收入之前，它为Reflection人工智能的20亿美元B轮融资开出了8亿美元的支票。“Jensen相信开源，坦率地说，是欧洲，”Bankiya谈到黄如何在世界各地的人工智能领域投入如此之多。在美国以外的其他地区，如欧洲，停止这种支持“将是一个巨大的资金和诚实的生态系统缺口，没有人能够填补。”英伟达在生态系统中是如此的主导者，有时感觉像是一个垄断。通过如此慷慨的支票支持如此多的公司，英伟达有效地锁定了这些初创公司使用其芯片，这有助于巩固其主导市场地位。如果该公司确实在创业投资方面变得更加保守，这可能会为竞争对手和较小的竞争对手留下更多空间。（巧合的是，谷歌本周在黄的英伟达事件中宣布了两款新芯片。）TechCrunch采访的一些投资者希望大型云公司和其他芯片制造商能够介入支持初创公司，填补黄可能留下的一些空白。例如，Battery Ventures的普通合伙人Dharmesh Thakker预计，大型科技公司的竞争对手将争夺Nvidia的投资冠军，“这取决于谁带来了与CUDA竞争的优秀产品、生态系统和社区的最佳组合，以及为生态系统提供资金的财力，”他说。CUDA是Nvidia的软件平台，使开发人员能够在其GPU上运行AI模型。虽然谷歌、亚马逊、微软和AMD等公司可能会支持成熟的初创公司推动其芯片和云的采用，但Thakker怀疑他们是否会像英伟达那样向营收前的基础设施初创公司投入数亿美元。
