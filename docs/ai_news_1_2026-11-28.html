Michael Nielsen took down his book on neural nets
I wrote this book between 2011 and 2015. At the time, I expected AGI systems based on neural networks to be many decades away, but to bring remarkable benefits for humanity. I also expected significant challenges, but believed it likely that, as with other technologies, those challenges would be addressed as the capabilities were developed. My views have changed. With the extraordinarily reckless behaviour of the frontier labs, the systems have raced ahead much faster than I expected 1 , giving humanity far too little time to meet the challenges. Although the systems genuinely will bring — indeed, already are bringing — many wonders, they also pose a significant risk of catastrophe. The frontier labs are knowingly endangering all of humanity. I've written about why I believe this at length , most recently in “Which Future?” . Two brief thoughts are below. I don't wish to help (even in a very small way) train people to contribute to this work, and so I've taken the book down. What follows are two brief remarks: one on the nature of the threat posed by ASI, and another on the ethical justification for working on frontier AI. The most common argument for AI existential risk, developed by Nick Bostrom and Eliezer Yudkowsky, is loss of control of the future to advanced AI systems 2 . I believe this concern should be understood as one instance of a more fundamental issue. That issue is whether we will discover technologies latent in nature which are easy and inexpensive to develop, catastrophic in outcome, and beyond our capacity to defend against. If such “progress” in technology were happening slowly, our social institutions might successfully develop defences. But it seems likely that ASI will cause a sudden explosion of such destructive technologies, with insufficient time to adapt. A historical example to help prime intuition is fire. It is easy to make and extremely difficult to defend against. We have a one-cent technology, the matchstick, which requires hundreds of billions of dollars each year to defend against, as well as a whole apparatus of social institutions, technology, infrastructure and law 3 . AI systems will enable technologies which are far more destructive than matches. Understandably, people skeptical of catastrophic risks want these supposed threats spelled out in detail. This is intrinsically difficult, because we can't reliably anticipate future science and technology. While it is common to speculate about biothreats, I expect the worst threats will be technologies we haven't yet imagined. Nature is, after all, more imaginative than we. Now suppose that ASI enables a 100-times speed-up in technological progress, without a matching change in the rate of social progress or social defenses. To get a sense of what the impact of such a speed-up would be, imagine that all the technological problems that occurred in the 20th century had occurred in the year 1901: the atomic bomb, the hydrogen bomb, the ICBM, chemical weapons, biological weapons, the CFC-caused ozone hole, sulfuric acid rain, climate change, and many more. While we've dealt well with some of these problems, it required decades of concerted work to develop the social institutions and agreements needed for an effective response. Again, defence is often much harder than offence, and relies on different systems. Of course, such a speed-up would also have brought the century's wonders all at once. But imagine another such set of equally difficult problems arising in 1902, and then 1903, and then 1904, and so on. I believe it would have made the world extraordinarily unstable and eventually liable to collapse. The issue with ASI, then, is the destructive power latent in nature. It doesn't matter whether that power is wielded by humans or by out-of-control machines 4 . I make this point because many people mistakenly believe that loss of control to such rogue machines is the fundamental issue. This leads them to treat AI safety as principally a technical problem, which can be addressed by “aligning” individual systems. Unfortunately, it cannot. Furthermore, such a view makes it seem as though the labs are the primary places where safety can be addressed. But such technical alignment is mostly market-supplied safety, which makes the systems more palatable to consumers and governments. It speeds development, and thus makes the fundamental underlying problem worse, while only addressing an (important) subsidiary problem. My view isn't popular, in part because it makes the problem of technology about making the entire world safe, not only about aligning particular AI systems. Indeed, even in a future where humanity no longer existed, the same problem would afflict any successor AI civilization: it would have to defend itself against these destructive capacities. Similarly, it afflicts any model of the future based on human uplift, through brain-computer interfaces and similar augmentation: the issue isn't which substrate intelligence runs on, but the dual-use nature of deep understanding of reality 5 . In some sense, it's a question of whether sentience in the cosmos is compatible with advanced understanding of nature. Of course, I hope it is compatible. But rushing the technology while the broader social response lags seems likely to result in failure on our particular planet. Many people inside the frontier AI labs genuinely believe AI systems may pose a catastrophic threat. Why then do they help build such systems? They often justify their participation by arguing that it's better they do it than someone else does.

Michael Nielsen取下了他关于神经网络的书
我在2011年至2015年间写了这本书。当时，我预计基于神经网络的AGI系统还需要几十年的时间，但会为人类带来显著的好处。我也预计会面临重大挑战，但我相信，与其他技术一样，随着能力的发展，这些挑战很可能会得到解决。我的观点已经改变了。由于前沿实验室异常鲁莽的行为，这些系统的速度比我预期的要快得多，给人类应对挑战的时间太少了。尽管这些系统确实会带来——事实上，已经带来了——许多奇迹，但它们也带来了巨大的灾难风险。边境实验室故意危害全人类。我写了很多关于为什么我相信这一点的文章，最近一篇是在《哪个未来？》中。下面是两个简短的想法。我不想帮助（即使是很小的方式）培训人们为这项工作做出贡献，所以我把这本书拿了下来。以下是两条简短的评论：一条是关于ASI构成的威胁的性质，另一条是研究前沿人工智能的伦理理由。尼克·博斯特罗姆和埃利泽·尤德考斯基提出的人工智能存在风险的最常见论点是，先进的人工智能系统将失去对未来的控制。我认为，这种担忧应该被理解为更根本问题的一个例子。这个问题是，我们是否会发现自然界中潜在的技术，这些技术易于开发且成本低廉，结果是灾难性的，超出了我们的防御能力。如果技术的这种“进步”进展缓慢，我们的社会机构可能会成功地发展防御。但ASI似乎很可能会导致这种破坏性技术的突然爆发，而没有足够的时间来适应。帮助主要直觉的一个历史例子是火。它很容易制作，但很难防御。我们有一种一分钱的技术，火柴棒，每年需要数千亿美元来防御，还有一整套社会机构、技术、基础设施和法律。人工智能系统将启用比比赛更具破坏性的技术。可以理解的是，对灾难性风险持怀疑态度的人希望详细说明这些所谓的威胁。这本质上是困难的，因为我们无法可靠地预测未来的科学技术。虽然人们经常猜测生物威胁，但我预计最严重的威胁将是我们尚未想象到的技术。毕竟，大自然比我们更有想象力。现在假设ASI使技术进步速度提高了100倍，而社会进步速度或社会防御没有相应的变化。为了了解这种加速的影响，想象一下，20世纪发生的所有技术问题都发生在1901年：原子弹、氢弹、洲际弹道导弹、化学武器、生物武器、氟氯化碳造成的臭氧层空洞、硫酸雨、气候变化等等。虽然我们很好地处理了其中一些问题，但需要几十年的协同工作来发展有效应对所需的社会制度和协议。同样，防御往往比进攻难得多，并且依赖于不同的系统。当然，这样的加速也会同时带来本世纪的奇迹。但想象一下，1902年、1903年、1904年等等，又出现了一系列同样困难的问题。我相信，这会使世界变得异常不稳定，最终有可能崩溃。因此，ASI的问题在于其潜在的破坏力。无论这种力量是由人类还是失控的机器掌握，都无关紧要。我提出这一点是因为许多人错误地认为，对这种流氓机器失去控制是根本问题。这导致他们将人工智能安全主要视为一个技术问题，可以通过“对齐”单个系统来解决。不幸的是，它不能。此外，这种观点似乎表明实验室是解决安全问题的主要场所。但这种技术一致性主要是市场提供的安全性，这使得这些系统更受消费者和政府的欢迎。它加速了发展，从而使根本的根本问题变得更糟，而只解决了一个（重要的）次要问题。我的观点不受欢迎，部分原因是它使技术问题变得关于使整个世界安全，而不仅仅是关于调整特定的人工智能系统。事实上，即使在人类不再存在的未来，同样的问题也会困扰任何后续的人工智能文明：它必须保护自己免受这些破坏性能力的侵害。同样，它通过脑机接口和类似的增强来影响任何基于人类提升的未来模型：问题不在于智能运行在哪个基础上，而在于对现实的深入理解的双重用途。从某种意义上说，这是一个宇宙中的感知是否与对自然的高级理解相容的问题。当然，我希望它是兼容的。但是，在更广泛的社会反应滞后的情况下匆忙使用技术似乎可能会导致我们这个星球上的失败。前沿人工智能实验室的许多人真的认为人工智能系统可能会构成灾难性的威胁。那么，他们为什么要帮助建立这样的系统呢？他们经常为自己的参与辩护，辩称自己比别人做得更好。
