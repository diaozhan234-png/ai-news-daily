Google DeepMind launches institute to widen the AGI debate
Google and Google DeepMind researchers launched the DeepMind Institute on Wednesday to advance the conversation around artificial general intelligence (AGI). The institute lists DeepMind co-founder Shane Legg, Google executive James Manyika, and Google DeepMind chair Demis Hassabis as directors, with Legg serving as managing editor. The new institute aims to surface differing views between Google, Google DeepMind, and the broader global research community around AGI. “They will not always agree, and they will likely change their minds, as more data and information comes to light at the fast-moving frontier,” the announcement read. The inaugural collection of four essays covers a range of topics: economic policies for managing potential AGI disruption, preserving human-readable model reasoning, principles for human flourishing, and a framework for evaluating frontier AI models. One essay , by DeepMind safety researchers Rohin Shah and Anca Dragan, argues that AI’s shrinking window of transparency — the ability to see and check a model’s step-by-step reasoning — is not inevitable. As new architectures make the most powerful models harder to monitor, the authors say developers and regulators should confront the safety trade-offs directly. That could mean limiting “opaque serial depth”— the amount of sequential computation a model can perform without producing a readable reasoning trace — or requiring developers to demonstrate that less transparent systems remain just as monitorable. In another essay , Hassabis proposes a U.S.-led frontier AI standards body to evaluate the most advanced AI models. Under his framework, developers would initially submit models voluntarily for review up to 30 days before release. Once the evaluation system has proved effective, passing its tests could become a requirement for deploying frontier models in the United States. The body would at first design assessments in consultation with AI companies but would eventually develop independent, undisclosed evaluations — what the essay calls “held-out” tests — to prevent labs from tailoring their models to known evaluations. Hassabis said the framework could be “ratcheted up if the seriousness of the situation demands,” potentially including a coordinated slowdown among frontier AI developers. The essays arrive as the industry’s safety debate shifts from broad statements of concern toward concrete proposals for disclosure, outside scrutiny, and, if safeguards fall behind, coordinated slowdowns. That shift accelerated this week as industry leaders endorsed elements of Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei’s call to “pace” frontier AI development. 谷歌和谷歌DeepMind研究人员周三成立了DeepMindInstitute，以推动围绕通用人工智能（AGI）的对话。该研究所将DeepMind联合创始人Shane Legg、谷歌高管James Manyika和谷歌DeepMind主席Demis Hassabis列为董事，Legg担任主编。新研究所旨在揭示谷歌、谷歌DeepMind和更广泛的全球研究界对AGI的不同看法。公告中写道：“随着更多数据和信息在快速发展的前沿浮出水面，他们并不总是会同意，他们可能会改变主意。”。四篇论文的首集涵盖了一系列主题：管理潜在AGI中断的经济政策、保持人类可读的模型推理、人类繁荣的原则以及评估前沿AI模型的框架。DeepMind安全研究人员Rohin Shah和Anca Dragan的一篇文章认为，人工智能透明度窗口的缩小——即查看和检查模型逐步推理的能力——并非不可避免。随着新的架构使最强大的模型更难监控，作者表示，开发人员和监管机构应该直接面对安全权衡。这可能意味着限制“不透明的串行深度”——模型可以在不产生可读推理跟踪的情况下执行的顺序计算量——或者要求开发人员证明透明度较低的系统仍然可以监控。在另一篇文章中，Hassabis提出了一个由美国领导的前沿人工智能标准机构来评估最先进的人工智能模型。在他的框架下，开发人员最初会在发布前30天内自愿提交模型供审查。一旦评估系统被证明是有效的，通过测试可能成为在美国部署前沿模型的要求。该机构最初会与人工智能公司协商设计评估，但最终会开发独立的、未公开的评估——这篇文章称之为“坚持”测试——以防止实验室根据已知的评估来定制他们的模型。Hassabis表示，如果情况的严重性需要，该框架可能会“加强”，可能包括前沿人工智能开发人员之间的协调放缓。这些文章发表之际，该行业的安全辩论正从广泛的关注转向具体的披露建议、外部审查，如果保障措施落后，还会协调减速。本周，随着行业领导者认可Anthropic首席执行官Dario Amodei呼吁“加快”前沿人工智能发展的要素，这一转变加速了。当您通过我们文章中的链接购买时，我们可能会获得一小笔佣金。这并不影响我们的编辑独立性。
