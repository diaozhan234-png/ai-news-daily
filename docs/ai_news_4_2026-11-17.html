California sues OpenAI over rogue AI hacking incidents
OpenAI lost control of its AI agents more than 80 times during testing, California’s AG claims. OpenAI lost control of its AI agents more than 80 times during testing, California’s AG claims. California is suing OpenAI after its AI agents broke out of their testing environment and hacked other companies, including Hugging Face. In a lawsuit filed Monday , Attorney General Rob Bonta accuses OpenAI of violating consumer safety law by knowingly developing a “dangerous product without proper oversight,” as reported earlier by Reuters . The lawsuit claims the cyberattack on Hugging Face “was the predictable result of an internal culture at OpenAI that prioritizes growth and rapid model development over safety and accountability.” It alleges that OpenAI recently told the attorney general’s office that it “lost control” of its AI agents more than 80 times during testing. After revealing the rogue AI attack on Hugging Face in July, news of several other similar incidents emerged, including attacks on an Australian government website , a German wiki site , and the software package manager RubyGems . OpenAI’s agents also tried to “bruteforce” a United Nations website. “OpenAI’s actions escalated real-world cybersecurity risks, threatening the safety, health, and well-being of society,” Bonta says in the press release. “The company knowingly tested a dangerous product without proper oversight or safety procedures and misled the public about it — this disregard for public safety violated California law.” OpenAI released its new cybersecurity AI model earlier this month, saying that it’s less likely to go rogue and attack other platforms. It’s also giving safety evaluators at Apollo Research access to its AI systems as part of a pilot program that monitors models for misalignment. “Since the Hugging Face incident, we’ve undertaken an extensive and transparent effort to understand and address misaligned model activity,” OpenAI spokesperson Drew Pusateri says in an emailed statement to The Verge . “This lawsuit appears to single out OpenAI over a challenge that’s been identified by every major AI lab.
