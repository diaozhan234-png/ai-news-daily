NYT demands Brave shut down its paywall blocker and end deals with AI firms
The New York Times and other newspaper companies sued Brave Software yesterday, alleging that the browser maker illegally bypasses paywalls and sells stolen news content to AI companies. “Brave is the supplier, dealer, and distributor of stolen copyrighted content for its own users and many of the most prominent GenAI companies, including Anthropic, Perplexity, Mistral, xAI, and Cohere,” the NYT alleged in a lawsuit filed in US District Court for the Southern District of New York. Citing third-party revenue estimates, the suit claimed that “Brave has made more than $100 million by copying and reselling publishers’ work without consent or permission,” while “publishers have received nothing.” The lawsuit said, “AI companies pay Brave to provide them with real-time online content from the Brave search index,” and use content from the Brave index “to train their own AI models” and “to respond to specific user queries.” The NYT lawsuit points to Brave’s website , which says the Brave Search API is “used by some of the biggest names in AI, including Cohere, Mistral, Perplexity, and You.com.” The lawsuit accused Brave of all the major types of copyright infringement: direct infringement, contributory infringement, and vicarious infringement. The lawsuit claims Brave infringes copyrights directly by scraping articles for its search index, for its “Ask Brave” AI search feature, and for the Brave API that “serves as an illicit source of unlicensed copyrighted material, including Times journalism, to other AI companies.” The NYT accused Brave of being “a contributory and vicarious infringer when it deliberately induces, facilitates, controls, and profits from its users’ and GenAI customers’ copying of the copyrighted content.” Finally, the lawsuit said Brave violated the Digital Millennium Copyright Act (DMCA) by circumventing technological measures the NYT and other content owners use to restrict access to their articles. “The Brave Browser includes a default setting that, when activated, causes the browser to go behind website paywalls and provide the paywalled content to the user,” the lawsuit said. The browser “blocks advertising that would otherwise appear on The Times’s website, and instead shows its own ads, offering its users a cut of the revenue even as it deprives The Times of digital advertising revenue that helps it fund the expensive effort of producing journalism,” the lawsuit said. When testing the Brave browser for Mac today, we found that the optional “paywall blocker” feature made it possible to read a paywalled New York Times article. The feature was enabled in the default settings and can be turned off. The NYT asked for a permanent injunction to stop Brave from continuing the allegedly illegal conduct. The lawsuit seeks statutory damages, actual damages, restitution of profits derived from alleged copyright infringement, and “restitution and disgorgement of all revenue and profits Brave obtained through its ad-substitution scheme.” A similar lawsuit was filed against Brave in the same court yesterday by the New York Daily News, Chicago Tribune, and Orlando Sentinel companies. “AI giants are now desperate to avoid getting caught red-handed stealing news from publishers, so Brave Software is selling them a back door to the journalism they know they have no right to,” said Steven Lieberman, a lawyer for the plaintiffs. “Buying stolen goods secondhand doesn’t make them legitimate. Brave must be held responsible for laundering pirated reporting for the AI industry.” We contacted Brave and will update this article if it provides a response. Perplexity, which is not a defendant in the Brave lawsuit but is facing a different lawsuit filed by the NYT, accused the news organization of “disinformation” in a statement provided to Ars and other news outlets. Perplexity’s statement did not identify any specific inaccuracies in the lawsuit against Brave. “The New York Times is overpaying its attorneys and underpaying its journalists. (Who could’ve easily identified the disinformation in this suit.),” Perplexity’s statement said. The NYT sued Perplexity in December 2025, alleging that its AI tool gives users “outputs that are identical or substantially similar to” NYT articles, including “verbatim or near-verbatim reproductions, summaries, or abridgements of the original content.” Perplexity’s response to that lawsuit said its answer engine only fetches content from copyrighted articles in reply to user queries. Perplexity argued that “developing an AI tool that can allegedly automatically fetch and provide specific content in response to user requests based on its pre-configuration and access to materials available on the Internet” is not “volitional conduct.” The NYT also sued OpenAI and Microsoft in December 2023 in a lawsuit alleging that OpenAI trains its AI with stolen news content. The lawsuit is heading toward a trial , with OpenAI arguing that its use of news articles is fair use under copyright law.

纽约时报要求Brave关闭其付费墙拦截器，并终止与人工智能公司的交易
《纽约时报》和其他报业公司昨日起诉Brave Software，指控该浏览器制造商非法绕过付费墙，将窃取的新闻内容出售给人工智能公司。《纽约时报》在向美国纽约南区地方法院提起的诉讼中称：“Brave是为其用户和许多最著名的GenAI公司（包括Anthropic、Perplexity、Mistral、xAI和Cohere）提供被盗版权内容的供应商、经销商和分销商。”《纽约时报》的诉讼指向Brave的网站，该网站称Brave search API“被人工智能领域的一些大牌使用，包括Cohere、Mistral、Perplexity和You.com”该诉讼指控Brave侵犯了所有主要类型的版权：直接侵权、共同侵权和替代侵权。该诉讼称，Brave通过为其搜索索引、“Ask Brave”人工智能搜索功能以及Brave API抓取文章，直接侵犯了版权，该API“为其他人工智能公司提供未经授权的版权材料，包括时报新闻报道”。《纽约时报》指控Brave“故意诱导、便利、控制用户和GenAI客户复制受版权保护的内容并从中获利”。最后，该诉讼称Brave规避了纽约时报和其他内容所有者限制访问其文章的技术措施，违反了《数字千年版权法》（DMCA）。诉讼称：“Brave浏览器包括一个默认设置，当激活时，会使浏览器越过网站付费墙，向用户提供付费墙内容。”。诉讼称，该浏览器“阻止了原本会出现在《泰晤士报》网站上的广告，而是显示了自己的广告，为用户提供了一部分收入，即使它剥夺了《泰晤士报》的数字广告收入，而数字广告收入有助于它为制作新闻的昂贵努力提供资金”。在今天测试适用于Mac的Brave浏览器时，我们发现可选的“付费墙拦截器”功能可以阅读《纽约时报》的付费墙文章。该功能在默认设置中启用，可以关闭。《纽约时报》要求永久禁令，以阻止Brave继续进行所谓的非法行为。该诉讼寻求法定损害赔偿、实际损害赔偿、归还因涉嫌侵犯版权而获得的利润，以及“归还和追缴Brave通过其广告替代计划获得的所有收入和利润”。昨天，《纽约每日新闻》、《芝加哥论坛报》和《奥兰多哨兵报》公司在同一法院对Brave提起了类似诉讼。原告律师史蒂文·利伯曼表示：“人工智能巨头现在迫切希望避免被抓到从出版商那里窃取新闻，所以勇敢软件公司向他们出售了一扇后门，让他们知道自己无权从事新闻工作。”。“购买二手赃物并不意味着它们是合法的。Brave必须对人工智能行业的盗版报告洗钱负责。”我们联系了Brave，如果它做出回应，我们将更新这篇文章。Perplexity不是Brave诉讼的被告，但正面临《纽约时报》提起的另一项诉讼，该公司在提供给Ars和其他新闻媒体的一份声明中指责该新闻机构“虚假信息”。Perplexity的声明没有指出对Brave的诉讼中有任何具体的不准确之处。“《纽约时报》向律师支付了过高的报酬，却向记者支付了过低的报酬。（谁能轻易识别出这起诉讼中的虚假信息。）”。《纽约时报》于2025年12月起诉了Perplexity，声称其人工智能工具为用户提供了“与《纽约邮报》文章相同或基本相似的输出”，包括“原始内容的逐字或近乎逐字的复制品、摘要或删节”。Perplexiity对该诉讼的回应称，其答案引擎仅在回答用户查询时从受版权保护的文章中获取内容。困惑认为，“开发一种人工智能工具，据称可以根据其预先配置和对互联网上可用材料的访问，自动获取和提供特定内容以响应用户请求”不是“自愿行为”。《纽约时报》还在2023年12月起诉了OpenAI和微软，指控OpenAI用被盗的新闻内容训练其人工智能。该诉讼正走向审判，OpenAI辩称，根据版权法，其对新闻文章的使用是合理使用。
