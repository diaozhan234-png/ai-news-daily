Texas halts data center connections to power grid amid overwhelming demand
Nowhere is the US data center boom bigger than in Texas. But less than a year after declaring Texas the “epicenter of AI development,” Governor Greg Abbott has declared a moratorium on all new power grid connections for data centers—at least until developers provide more information about their projects’ potential impacts on the grid and communities. The Republican governor directed regulators in an August 3 announcement at the Public Utility Commission of Texas and the grid operators at the Electric Reliability Council of Texas (ERCOT) to perform a “comprehensive verification and audit of all data centers advancing through ERCOT’s interconnection process.” As an independent system operator, ERCOT oversees a power grid that operates separately from the rest of the United States and provides services to most of Texas. Texas has aggressively courted data center development with its availability of cheap land and relatively abundant energy resources, along with offering state incentives, like tax breaks and fewer regulations. That puts the state on track to surpass Virginia in becoming the largest US data center market . But the recent AI boom and the accompanying frenzy of data center development threaten to overwhelm the Texas grid on paper, despite the state leading the country in adding new power generation . The ERCOT interconnection queue currently includes more than 1,800 projects representing over 474 gigawatts’ worth of requests to connect to the Texas grid—more than five times Texas’ record peak electricity demand —and about 90 percent of those power connection requests come from data centers. “That unprecedented load growth could endanger the reliability and stability of the Texas electric grid,” according to the statement from Abbott’s office. Many of those data center projects may never materialize for various reasons. But ERCOT has still forecast that data center demand and other factors could drive statewide electricity demand to double the current demand record by 2032, according to The Texas Tribune . The AI boom has led to additional consequences for Texas. A data center tax break that originally passed with bipartisan support in 2014 has ballooned into more than $1 billion in tax breaks annually for data center developers—and The Texas Tribune reported the state government estimates it could lose $3.2 billion in sales tax revenue over the next two years. The new directive from Abbott requires audits of data center projects that include how much data centers would depend on the ERCOT grid for power, along with obtaining projections of data centers’ annual and peak electricity consumption. The directive further requires information on how much individual data center projects depend on state financial assistance, along with details on each data center project’s ownership and controlling interests. Abbott also directed state regulators and grid operators to discover the extent to which data centers’ cooling systems and water usage may draw upon local water supplies needed by local communities. However, the governor’s directive does not mention the fact that data centers often use much more water through their power generation sources than directly through their cooling systems—a fact highlighted by researchers like Shaolei Ren at the University of California, Riverside. Texas communities like Corpus Christi have already been grappling with a yearslong drought. But their water woes have been compounded by both climate change and large energy and chemical companies’ operations that have “sucked the region dry,” according to San Antonio News . A Sierra Club report looking at federal data showed how Texas natural gas plants used 56 billion gallons of water in 2024, while coal plants consumed an additional 34 billion gallons, and nuclear plants used 26 billion gallons. By comparison, Texas data centers directly used 8 billion gallons of water for cooling purposes, but many such data centers rely on water-intensive power generation sources. Abbott’s directive also seeks to increase scrutiny of measures that data centers are taking to “reduce impacts on neighboring property owners and communities, including noise mitigation, light controls, setbacks, traffic improvements, emergency response coordination and other community protection measures.” But the directive conspicuously neglects to mention local air pollution and greenhouse gas emissions associated with data centers. Nonprofit newsroom Floodlight has reported that AI companies used a local permit loophole in Texas to install gas-powered turbines and backup diesel generators on site at new data center campuses—all without requiring more extensive environmental reviews or outreach to local communities. Notably, the Texas pause on data center grid connections does not apply to data center projects that are building their own on-site power generation to get up and running as fast as possible. That “behind-the-meter power” strategy has become increasingly common among tech and AI companies like Meta, Microsoft, Amazon, Oracle, OpenAI, and Anthropic, according to research by market intelligence platform CleanView . Such behind-the-meter power typically relies on natural gas. But with lengthy order backlogs for combined-cycle gas turbines, CleanView says data center developers are turning to alternative sources, such as “mobile gas generators strapped to semitrucks” and “aeroderivative turbines originally designed for aircraft and warships.” Texas leads all US states in total announced behind-the-meter capacity for data centers by having 40 gigawatts of such announced capacity. That factor is driven by the Texas Permian Basin gas supply and “extensive pipeline infrastructure” along with the “state’s permissive regulatory environment,” according to CleanView. Despite the oversight gaps, the Texas moratorium on data center approvals “throws a massive wrench into ERCOT’s plans to bring data centers online,” according to E&E News .

得克萨斯州在需求旺盛的情况下停止了数据中心与电网的连接
美国数据中心的繁荣在德克萨斯州最为显著。但在宣布德克萨斯州为“人工智能发展的中心”不到一年后，州长格雷格·阿博特宣布暂停数据中心的所有新电网连接——至少在开发商提供更多关于其项目对电网和社区潜在影响的信息之前。这位共和党州长在8月3日得克萨斯州公用事业委员会的一份公告中指示监管机构和得克萨斯州电力可靠性委员会（ERCOT）的电网运营商对“通过ERCOT互联过程推进的所有数据中心进行全面验证和审计”。作为一家独立的系统运营商，ERCOT监督着一个与美国其他地区分开运营的电网，并为得克萨斯州大部分地区提供服务。得克萨斯州凭借其廉价的土地和相对丰富的能源资源，以及税收减免和更少的监管等州激励措施，积极推动数据中心的发展。这使该州有望超越弗吉尼亚州，成为美国最大的数据中心市场。但最近的人工智能热潮和随之而来的数据中心开发热潮有可能在纸面上压倒德克萨斯州的电网，尽管该州在增加新的发电量方面领先全国。ERCOT互联队列目前包括1800多个项目，代表了超过474吉瓦的连接到德克萨斯州电网的请求，是德克萨斯州创纪录的峰值电力需求的五倍多，其中约90%的电力连接请求来自数据中心。雅培办公室的声明称：“这种前所未有的负荷增长可能会危及德克萨斯州电网的可靠性和稳定性。”。由于各种原因，许多数据中心项目可能永远不会实现。但据《德克萨斯论坛报》报道，ERCOT仍预测，到2032年，数据中心需求和其他因素可能会推动全州电力需求翻一番。人工智能的繁荣给德克萨斯州带来了额外的后果。最初于2014年在两党支持下通过的数据中心税收减免已激增至每年为数据中心开发商提供超过10亿美元的税收减免，《德克萨斯论坛报》报道称，州政府估计，未来两年可能会损失32亿美元的销售税收入。Abbott的新指令要求对数据中心项目进行审计，包括数据中心在多大程度上依赖ERCOT电网供电，以及获得数据中心年度和峰值用电量的预测。该指令还要求提供有关单个数据中心项目在多大程度上依赖于国家财政援助的信息，以及每个数据中心项目的所有权和控制权的详细信息。Abbott还指示州监管机构和电网运营商了解数据中心的冷却系统和用水在多大程度上可能会占用当地社区所需的当地供水。然而，州长的指示没有提到这样一个事实，即数据中心通过其发电来源使用的水通常比直接通过其冷却系统使用的水多得多——这一事实得到了加州大学河滨分校的研究人员任少磊的强调。像科珀斯克里斯蒂这样的德克萨斯州社区已经在努力应对长达一年的干旱。但据《圣安东尼奥新闻》报道，气候变化和大型能源和化工公司的运营“使该地区干涸”，加剧了他们的水资源问题。塞拉俱乐部的一份研究联邦数据的报告显示，2024年，德克萨斯州天然气厂消耗了560亿加仑的水，而燃煤电厂消耗了340亿加仑，核电厂消耗了260亿加仑。相比之下，德克萨斯州的数据中心直接使用了80亿加仑的水进行冷却，但许多这样的数据中心依赖于水密集型发电资源。Abbott的指令还试图加强对数据中心为“减少对邻近业主和社区的影响，包括噪音缓解、灯光控制、挫折、交通改善、应急响应协调和其他社区保护措施”而采取的措施的审查。但该指令明显忽略了与数据中心相关的当地空气污染和温室气体排放。非营利组织新闻编辑室Floodlight报道称，人工智能公司利用德克萨斯州的一个当地许可漏洞，在新的数据中心园区现场安装燃气轮机和备用柴油发电机，而无需进行更广泛的环境审查或与当地社区进行外联。值得注意的是，德克萨斯州暂停数据中心电网连接并不适用于正在建设自己的现场发电设施以尽快启动和运行的数据中心项目。根据市场情报平台CleanView的研究，这种“幕后力量”战略在Meta、微软、亚马逊、甲骨文、OpenAI和Anthropic等科技和人工智能公司中越来越普遍。这种表后功率通常依赖于天然气。但是，CleanView表示，由于联合循环燃气轮机的订单积压时间很长，数据中心开发商正在转向替代来源，例如“绑在半卡车上的移动式燃气发电机”和“最初为飞机和军舰设计的航改涡轮机”。得克萨斯州以40吉瓦的公布容量领先于美国所有州。CleanView表示，这一因素是由德克萨斯州二叠纪盆地的天然气供应和“广泛的管道基础设施”以及“该州宽松的监管环境”推动的。据E&E News报道，尽管存在监管漏洞，但德克萨斯州暂停数据中心审批“给ERCOT将数据中心上线的计划带来了巨大的阻碍”。
