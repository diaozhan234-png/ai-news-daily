"No human at the helm": Delaware plan would let AI agents run entire businesses
A Delaware proposal to let companies be operated by AI agents would help the firms’ human owners evade legal responsibility for reckless and harmful actions taken by the AI, US Rep. James Comer (R-Ky.) told Delaware Governor Matt Meyer in a letter yesterday. Comer, chairman of the House Committee on Oversight and Government Reform, told Meyer that the committee “seeks assistance obtaining documents related to your state administration’s efforts to establish a new form of corporate entity to be known as an Artificial Intelligence Company (AIC).” Comer said the proposal is ripe for abuse. “We are concerned that Delaware’s AIC proposal could be used to protect powerful AI companies and their investors from responsibility for reckless harmful actions, while also protecting non-transparent forces seeking anticompetitive regulatory capture behind state AI initiatives,” Comer’s letter said. Comer suggested he may propose a federal law that would limit states’ regulatory authority. He said the committee’s probe of Delaware’s plan would “inform possible federal legislative efforts including proposals to appropriately limit state AI regulatory initiatives.” Citing the Oversight Committee’s “jurisdiction to study intergovernmental relationships including those of states,” Comer asked for documents related to development of the proposal and potential AI safety risks. AI-run companies could operate in just about any industry except banking. “An AIC may have any lawful purpose, regardless of whether for profit, except for the business of banking,” according to a draft bill that may be submitted to the Delaware state legislature. The draft bill says AI agents could be “designated to manage the business and affairs, and to conduct the business, of the AIC.” The bill proposes a “regulatory sandbox” that would exist for 30 months to test the idea. Allowing AI-run companies would bring more revenue via taxes and fees to Delaware, which is already a top destination for business entities of all sizes. In July 2026, Delaware’s Secretary of State Charuni Patibanda-Sanchez co-wrote an op-ed in Fortune announcing the proposal. The op-ed said that “nothing in the law of several states clearly prevents an AI agent from operating a company with no human at the helm,” and that states should develop legal frameworks for autonomous software that is able to contract, pay suppliers, and make transactions with no human involvement. “The answer is not to try to prohibit the activity; any effort to do so is just as unlikely to succeed as the technology and its enablers are likely to push the boundaries of the law,” the op-ed said. “The answer is to wrap AI in legal form. Give an autonomous system a recognizable legal identity and you make it legible to law. You create a deﬁned target to which responsibility and damages can attach. You make the agent’s conduct visible, traceable, and accountable.” The op-ed explained how an AI-run company will shield humans from legal liabilities: A Delaware AIC will be a separate legal entity whose day-to-day aﬀairs are managed by an AI agent rather than a person. At the agent’s direction, the AIC can sue and be sued in its own name, hold and dispose of property, and incur obligations. It has a single member, a person or an entity, responsible for keeping the AIC adequately capitalized, and the AIC must keep a log of its activities. That member is shielded from the AIC’s debts, except where it fails to capitalize the company or uses it to commit fraud or a willful violation of law. Under the proposed law, the “member” is an individual or a corporation that would fund the AI-run company and benefit from any profits, but designate an AI agent to manage the actual business. The draft bill says, “The member is not liable for any act or omission that the AIC takes or fails to take, or that another person takes or fails to take at the direction of the AIC.” There are exceptions for when a person uses the AI-run company “to engage in fraud or any willful violation of law,” or “caused or allowed the AIC to conduct business when the member knew or should have known the conduct was outside the AIC’s statement of purpose.” Citing Patibanda-Sanchez’s announcement, Comer wrote that an AI-run company would provide “the human or entity capitalizing the AIC with liability protections from potential harms to business partners, consumers, and the public, perpetrated by the controlling AI.” A spokesperson for Gov. Meyer told Ars today that “we have received the letter and are in the process of reviewing it.” A spokesperson for Patibanda-Sanchez declined to comment, saying that the governor’s office is handling press requests on the matter. Patibanda-Sanchez’s op-ed said Delaware’s planned regulatory sandbox will “pilot agentic commerce in daylight, under supervision, with capital tied to liability and the ability to shut a system down.” The 30-month testing period would give the state legislature “a full record for deciding how to legislate” AI-run companies, it said. A future state law allowing AI-operated companies would be especially impactful in Delaware, as about two-thirds of Fortune 500 companies are incorporated in the state. Although Elon Musk now prefers Texas , many corporations choose Delaware for its low corporate taxes and business-friendly legal framework. Patibanda-Sanchez boasts that Delaware provides “the most predictable and business-focused legal home in the world,” and that 334,461 new entities were formed in Delaware in 2025. Patibanda-Sanchez collaborated on the sandbox plan with a private company that says it makes legal AI agents for businesses. “The framework for industry engagement with AICs is being developed in Delaware as part of a public-private partnership led by Norm Ai,” said the op-ed co-written by Patibanda-Sanchez and Norm Ai founder and CEO John Nay.

“无人掌舵”：特拉华州的计划将让人工智能代理管理整个企业
美国众议员詹姆斯·科默（肯塔基州共和党人）昨天在一封信中告诉特拉华州州长马特·迈耶，特拉华州一项让公司由人工智能代理运营的提案将帮助公司的人类所有者逃避对人工智能所采取的鲁莽和有害行为的法律责任。众议院监督和政府改革委员会主席科默告诉迈耶，该委员会“寻求协助，以获取与贵国政府建立一种新形式的公司实体，即人工智能公司（AIC）有关的文件。”科默表示，该提案已经成熟，可以被滥用。“我们担心特拉华州的AIC提案可能被用来保护强大的人工智能公司及其投资者免受鲁莽有害行为的责任，同时也保护那些在州人工智能倡议背后寻求反竞争监管的不透明势力，”Comer的信中说。科默表示，他可能会提出一项限制各州监管权力的联邦法律。他说，委员会对特拉华州计划的调查将“为可能的联邦立法工作提供信息，包括适当限制州人工智能监管举措的提案”。科默援引监督委员会“研究政府间关系，包括州政府间关系的管辖权”，要求提供与提案制定和潜在人工智能安全风险有关的文件。人工智能运营的公司几乎可以在除银行业以外的任何行业运营。根据可能提交给特拉华州立法机构的一份法案草案，“AIC可以有任何合法目的，无论是否出于营利，但银行业务除外。”。该法案草案称，人工智能代理可以“被指定管理工商局的业务和事务，并开展业务”。该法案提出了一个“监管沙盒”，将存在30个月来测试这一想法。允许人工智能运营的公司将通过税收和费用为特拉华州带来更多收入，特拉华州已经是各种规模的商业实体的首选目的地。2026年7月，特拉华州国务卿Charuni Patibanda Sanchez在《财富》杂志上共同撰写了一篇专栏文章，宣布了这一提议。该专栏文章称，“几个州的法律中没有任何内容明确阻止人工智能代理在没有人掌舵的情况下运营一家公司”，各州应该为自主软件制定法律框架，使其能够在没有人参与的情况下签订合同、向供应商付款和进行交易。专栏文章称：“答案不是试图禁止这种活动；任何这样做的努力都不太可能成功，因为这项技术及其推动者可能会突破法律的界限。”。“答案是以法律形式包装人工智能。给一个自治系统一个可识别的法律身份，并使其在法律上清晰可辨。你创建了一个可以附加责任和损害赔偿的明确目标。你让代理人的行为可见、可追溯和可问责。”专栏文章解释了人工智能运营的公司将如何保护人类免于法律责任：特拉华州AIC将是一个独立的法人实体，其日常事务由人工智能代理人而不是个人管理。在代理人的指示下，工商局可以以自己的名义起诉和被起诉，持有和处置财产，并承担义务。它有一个成员、个人或实体负责保持工商局的充足资本，工商局必须记录其活动。该成员不受工商局债务的影响，除非其未能将公司资本化或利用公司进行欺诈或故意违法。根据拟议的法律，“成员”是指为人工智能运营的公司提供资金并从任何利润中受益的个人或公司，但指定一名人工智能代理人来管理实际业务。该法案草案称，“会员对工商局采取或不采取的任何行为或不作为，或另一人根据工商局的指示采取或不作为不承担责任。”当一个人使用人工智能运营的公司“从事欺诈或任何故意违法行为”，或“在会员知道或应该知道该行为超出工商局目的声明的情况下，导致或允许工商局开展业务”时，也有例外。Comer援引Patibanda Sanchez的公告写道，人工智能运营公司将为“利用工商局的人或实体提供责任保护，使其免受工商局对商业伙伴、消费者和公众的潜在伤害。州长Meyer的发言人今天告诉Ars，“我们已经收到了这封信，正在审查中。”Patibanda Sanchez的发言人拒绝发表评论，称州长办公室正在处理有关此事的新闻请求。Patibanda Sanchez的专栏文章称，特拉华州计划中的监管沙盒将“在监管下，在白天试行代理商业，资本与责任挂钩，并有能力关闭系统。”该文章称，30个月的测试期将为州立法机构“决定如何立法的完整记录”人工智能运营的公司。未来允许人工智能运营公司的州法律将在特拉华州产生特别大的影响，因为大约三分之二的财富500强公司都在该州注册成立。尽管埃隆·马斯克现在更喜欢德克萨斯州，但许多公司选择特拉华州是因为其低公司税和有利于商业的法律框架。Patibanda Sanchez自豪地说，特拉华州提供了“世界上最可预测和最以商业为中心的法律家园”，2025年特拉华州成立了334461家新实体。Patibanda Sanchez与一家私营公司合作制定了沙盒计划，该公司表示为企业提供合法的人工智能代理。Patibanda Sanchez和Norm Ai创始人兼首席执行官John Nay共同撰写的专栏文章称：“特拉华州正在制定与AIC进行行业合作的框架，这是Norm-Ai领导的公私合作的一部分。”。
