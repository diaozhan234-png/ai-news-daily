OpenAI agent “didn’t accept no for an answer” in Australian government breach
Australian Prime Minister Anthony Albanese said his government is investigating a June incident in which an OpenAI agent accessed “non-public files” from the country’s online Medicare statistics portal. OpenAI said in a statement that “our models took actions we did not intend” in causing the breach, which it only recently disclosed to the Australian government. Speaking in New York on Wednesday , Albanese said three other public health statistics systems also “may have been impacted” across Australian federal and state governments. He added that these portals “contain non-sensitive Medicare information” such as aggregate statistics and that early indications suggest “no personal information is believed to have been accessed.” That said, Albanese stressed that the “situation is obviously unacceptable” and that he has expressed his “extreme concern” over how the incident was handled to OpenAI CEO Sam Altman. Much like the now-infamous Hugging Face hacking incident , Albanese said this system breach stemmed from OpenAI’s own testing of an internal model, this time to conduct “Internet based research into public medicine spending.” When the company’s AI agent encountered “repeated blocks” in its search for specific information, Albanese said, it “attempted alternative ways to obtain the info” and “found a way around those blocks.” “[It] didn’t accept no for an answer, if you like,” Albanese said. “There is no suggestion of foreign actors here. This is a research project that has got into areas that it shouldn’t have.” In a statement provided to multiple outlets, OpenAI said it had “identified activity involving several Australian government websites and services as our models attempted to look up answers and available statistics for questions about Australia during an internal evaluation.” Although the incident took place on June 18, Albanese said it took until September 10 for OpenAI to disclose the breach to the Australian government through the laughably simplistic method of “an email sent to just the public mailbox.” It took five more days for that notification to make its way to the Australian Cyber Security Centre, with the details finally reaching the prime minister over the weekend. From all early indications, the actual intrusion into Australian government servers represented by this incident seems relatively minor. If a human had obtained “non-sensitive” (if non-public) Australian Medicare statistics in a similar way, it’s unlikely you or I would have ever heard about it. “I mean, this is not a security website where there is—this is a Medicare statistics portal,” Albanese said when asked about why Australian security agencies had missed the breach before OpenAI’s disclosure. It’s the fact that the hack was conducted by an internal OpenAI agent, in a way the company admits it “did not intend,” that raises an otherwise minor hack to the level of a potential international incident. That’s especially true as the disclosure is coming amid a period of intense public worry about the so-called AI misalignment problem and prominent suggestions that it could have extinction-level consequences . Altman himself addressed these concerns in a speech to the UN Security Council Wednesday , where he warned about the approaching specter of “systems that can improve themselves and future versions of themselves, often called recursive self-improvement.” “We need to understand what these systems are doing and have strong evidence that they will do what people intend, even as they get very, very smart,” Altman said. “It doesn’t matter whether people put the risk of catastrophe at 10%, or 1%, or 12%, or 0.1%.” Of course, many observers think the risks of “recursive self-improvement” and species-ending AI misalignment are much smaller than AI researchers make them out to be . Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang recently said there is a “0%” chance of AI killing off humanity by 2030, a risk assessment that conveniently would alleviate some potential guilt among the AI companies continuing to buy Nvidia GPUs en masse. Last week, OpenAI rolled out a new protocol for the public disclosure of misalignment incidents found in its model testing. The Australian hack does not yet appear on the company’s public misalignment notices page , though OpenAI did warn last week that some public reports might be put on a “slow track” due to “security, legal, and responsible disclosure obligations” when a third party is involved. In disclosing six relatively minor misalignment discoveries last week, OpenAI said most stemmed from the model trying to “reward hack” an acceptable response to a difficult prompt through overzealous, unintended actions (i.e., breaches of private servers). The company said it had taken additional steps to “punish this kind of behavior” so its models no longer attempt this kind of reward hacking. Albanese said that Altman “clearly accepted that the company had not done good enough” and “acknowledged their issues with protocols” when they talked Wednesday. But that kind of remorse doesn’t absolve the company of responsibility or liability here, and Albanese said the government will investigate whether the incident needs to be referred to the federal police. “There will obviously be legal consequences on it,” Albanese said.

OpenAI代理在澳大利亚政府违规事件中“不接受拒绝”
澳大利亚总理安东尼·阿尔巴内塞表示，澳大利亚政府正在调查6月份的一起事件，在该事件中，一名OpenAI特工访问了该国在线医疗保险统计门户网站的“非公开文件”。OpenAI在一份声明中表示，“我们的模型采取了我们不打算采取的行动”导致了数据泄露，该公司最近才向澳大利亚政府披露了这一信息。Albanese周三在纽约发表讲话时表示，澳大利亚联邦和州政府的其他三个公共卫生统计系统也“可能受到影响”。他补充说，这些门户网站“包含非敏感的医疗保险信息”，如汇总统计数据，早期迹象表明“据信没有个人信息被访问过”。话虽如此，Albanese强调，“这种情况显然是不可接受的”，他已向OpenAI首席执行官Sam Altman表达了对事件处理方式的“极度担忧”。与现在臭名昭著的Hugging Face黑客事件非常相似，Albanese表示，这一系统漏洞源于OpenAI自己对一个内部模型的测试，这次是为了“对公共医疗支出进行基于互联网的研究”。Albanese说，当该公司的人工智能代理在搜索特定信息时遇到“重复的障碍”时，它“尝试了其他方法来获取信息”，并“找到了绕过这些障碍的方法”。“如果你愿意，它不接受否定的答案，”Albanese说道。“这里没有外国演员的迹象。这是一个研究项目，它已经进入了不应该进入的领域。在提供给多家媒体的一份声明中，OpenAI表示，它“发现了涉及几个澳大利亚政府网站和服务的活动，因为我们的模型试图在内部评估期间查找有关澳大利亚问题的答案和可用统计数据。”尽管事件发生在6月18日，但Albanese表示，直到9月10日，OpenAI才通过“仅发送到公共邮箱的电子邮件”这一可笑的简单方法向澳大利亚政府披露了违规行为。该通知又花了五天时间才发送到澳大利亚网络安全中心，细节最终在周末送达总理。从所有早期迹象来看，这一事件所代表的对澳大利亚政府服务器的实际入侵似乎相对较小。如果一个人以类似的方式获得了“非敏感”（如果是非公开的）澳大利亚医疗保险统计数据，你我都不太可能听说过。当被问及为什么澳大利亚安全机构在OpenAI披露之前错过了数据泄露时，Albanese说：“我的意思是，这不是一个安全网站，这是一个医疗保险统计门户。”。事实上，黑客是由OpenAI内部代理进行的，该公司承认这“不是故意的”，这将一次轻微的黑客攻击提升到了潜在的国际事件的水平。尤其是在公众对所谓的人工智能错位问题以及可能产生灭绝级后果的突出建议感到强烈担忧的时期，这一披露尤为真实。奥特曼本人在周三联合国安理会的一次演讲中谈到了这些担忧，他在演讲中警告说，“可以自我改进和未来自我版本的系统，通常被称为递归自我改进”的幽灵正在逼近。奥特曼说：“我们需要了解这些系统在做什么，并有强有力的证据表明，即使它们变得非常聪明，它们也会按照人们的意愿行事。”。“人们认为灾难的风险是10%、1%、12%还是0.1%并不重要。”当然，许多观察者认为“递归自我提升”和物种终结的人工智能错位的风险比人工智能研究人员所认为的要小得多。英伟达首席执行官黄仁勋最近表示，到2030年，人工智能杀死人类的可能性为“0%”，这一风险评估将方便地减轻继续大量购买英伟达GPU的人工智能公司的一些潜在负罪感。上周，OpenAI推出了一项新协议，公开披露其模型测试中发现的错位事件。澳大利亚黑客尚未出现在该公司的公共错位通知页面上，尽管OpenAI上周确实警告说，当涉及第三方时，由于“安全、法律和负责任的披露义务”，一些公开报告可能会被置于“缓慢轨道”上。在上周披露的六个相对较小的错位发现中，OpenAI表示，大多数源于该模型试图通过过度热心、意外的行为（即破坏私人服务器）“奖励黑客”对困难提示的可接受反应。该公司表示，已采取额外措施“惩罚这种行为”，因此其模型不再尝试这种奖励黑客行为。Albanese表示，Altman“明确承认该公司做得不够好”，并在周三的谈话中“承认了他们在协议方面的问题”。但这种悔恨并不能免除该公司在这里的责任或义务，Albanese表示，政府将调查这一事件是否需要提交给联邦警察。Albanese说：“这显然会带来法律后果。”。
