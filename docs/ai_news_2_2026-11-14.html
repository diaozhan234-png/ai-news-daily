ChatGPT Wallet lets OpenAI’s Dots agents make purchases for you
OpenAI says ChatGPT Wallet won’t share your actual credit card info with its AI agents.﻿ OpenAI says ChatGPT Wallet won’t share your actual credit card info with its AI agents.﻿ After launching its Dots AI agents platform earlier this year, OpenAI is launching a wallet for the agents. ChatGPT Wallet is rolling out starting today for ChatGPT Pro subscribers in the US aged 18 and older using a US billing address and “eligible US-issued American Express, Chase, Mastercard and Visa cards.” After adding a card to the wallet, your Dots agents can use it to buy things in response to your prompts, but they’ll check in with you each time before checking out. OpenAI’s support page says every purchase with ChatGPT Wallet requires a security check with biometric verification like Face ID or a fingerprint scan. After you review the AI agent’s purchase and sign off on it, OpenAI says that, similar to using tap to pay , Apple Pay , or Google Wallet , it substitutes your credit card details with a payment token, so your actual card info isn’t shared with your Dots agent or the business you’re buying from. Eventually, OpenAI says it expects “users to be able to delegate purchases entirely to their agent by setting clear parameters—such as spending limits, purchase categories, or time periods—and pre-approve those purchases.” ChatGPT Wallet might not work everywhere. Some retailers have restrictions on AI agents or block them altogether. Earlier this year, Amazon blocked Meta’s Muse agents.
