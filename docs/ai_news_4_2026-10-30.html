Counting the Bandages: On measuring the time that AI saves
arXiv:2610.35942v1 Announce Type: new Abstract: I consider what it means for AI assistance to save time. I contrast a stopwatch view, on which time saved is whatever the clock records, with a ledger view, on which it is a counterfactual that has to be read next to the errors the work lets through, and I argue for the latter. The argument has four parts. I define a model-free pair of quantities, time saved and excess escaped defects, and one decision number built from them: the break-even defect cost. I give a baseline model of checking in which, until the model is good enough not to check, a calibrated worker's escaped-defect rate does not depend on how good the model is; checking time grows with the logarithm of the model's error rate; and over-trust buys minutes on the clock at a larger cost in total minutes. I show what randomized studies can and cannot identify, and I replicate METR's developer study from its public data: the same 246 issues support a slowdown of 19% or 24% depending on the estimand (34% without adjusting for a chance imbalance), and AI helped on somewhere between 3% and 82% of them. Finally, I restate the published record, with stated bounds in place of imported point assumptions. In GDPval's own review-every-output workflow, review time alone caps GPT-5's speedup at 3.6x; the consultants outside the frontier were faster, and more often wrong. Last, I test two of the model's predictions in a small pre-specified experiment on AI reviewers checking AI drafts of the same kind of brief, built from SEC filings made after the models' training data were collected. Told the drafter's true error rate, the reviewer checked more as it rose, but all-or-nothing, switching near where its prompt's costs break even. Told that the drafter erred ten times less often than it did, it checked 7 fewer of 9 figures and let a wrong figure through in 22.5% of briefs instead of none: faster and worse, entirely through reduced effort.

数绷带：衡量人工智能节省的时间
arXiv:2610.3942v1公告类型：new摘要：我考虑人工智能辅助节省时间意味着什么。我对比了秒表视图和分类账视图，前者节省的时间取决于时钟记录的时间，后者是一个反事实，必须在工作中出现的错误旁边阅读，我支持后者。这个论点有四个部分。我定义了一对无模型的数量、节省的时间和多余的逃逸缺陷，以及一个由它们构建的决策数字：盈亏平衡缺陷成本。我给出了一个检查的基线模型，在该模型足够好之前，校准工人的逃逸缺陷率并不取决于模型有多好；检查时间随着模型误差率的对数而增长；过度信任会以更高的总分钟成本购买时钟上的分钟数。我展示了随机研究可以识别和不能识别的内容，并从其公开数据中复制了METR的开发者研究：同样的246个问题支持19%或24%的放缓，具体取决于估计值（在不调整机会不平衡的情况下为34%），人工智能在其中的3%到82%之间有所帮助。最后，我重申了已发布的记录，用规定的界限代替了导入的点假设。在GDPval自己对每个输出工作流的审查中，仅审查时间就将GPT-5的加速限制在3.6倍；边境以外的顾问更快，也更经常出错。最后，我在一个预先指定的小型实验中测试了模型的两个预测，该实验由人工智能审查人员检查了基于模型训练数据收集后美国证券交易委员会文件构建的同类简报的人工智能草稿。在告知起草者的真实错误率后，审阅者随着错误率的上升进行了更多的检查，但要么全部检查，要么什么都不检查，在提示成本接近平衡的地方进行切换。当被告知起草者的错误率比实际情况低十倍时，它检查了9个数字中的7个，并在22.5%的简报中让错误的数字通过，而不是没有通过：更快更糟，完全是通过减少工作量。
