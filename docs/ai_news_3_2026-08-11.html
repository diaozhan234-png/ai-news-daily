With new open models, Meta pitches another reboot of its struggling AI strategy
Meta has announced its intention to focus on open-weight large language models. Additionally, the company announced the release of an open model called Muse Glimmer and a promise to open the weights for Muse Spark 1.2, its more powerful model, in the next few weeks. Alongside these announcements, Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg published a more than 6,000-word essay outlining the company’s philosophy about AI systems and governance moving forward. The essay aims to differentiate Meta from companies like OpenAI and Anthropic, which develop proprietary models and which have lobbied the US government for help competing against large-scale distillation—which involves using an existing model to train a new one—or open-weight models by Chinese labs. Muse Glimmer is a 30 billion parameter model with a 128,000-token context window by default. It is distilled from Muse Spark, the larger and more capable model that Meta launched earlier this year. Glimmer is meant to run on users’ local machines, rather than via a cloud service or an API. Glimmer’s weights are open source under the Apache 2.0 license. Muse Spark was introduced in April as a closed, proprietary, frontier-class model—Meta’s first major model release after a significant shake-up of the company’s AI teams last year, and a departure from its focus on models that are, by some definition, open. When Meta released Muse Spark 1.1 in July, it introduced its first paid service—again, a departure from its previous strategy. Muse Spark 1.2 was released on August 5 and was accompanied by Muse Code, a terminal coding agent. Developers have generally found that Muse Code doesn’t quite match the frontier models from Anthropic or OpenAI in capability, but it competes well on cost—meaning it has similar positioning to many open-weight models from Chinese labs. As a smaller model designed to run on consumer GPUs, Muse Glimmer won’t compete on that level at all—but it reflects a growing movement to bring some inference to local devices to reduce reliance and spending on the models produced by the big labs like Anthropic and OpenAI. Alongside the model releases, Mark Zuckerberg is credited as the author of a lengthy open letter that details Meta’s corporate strategy with AI, and a broader argument for how AI should be developed, distributed, and regulated. It follows several statements by other Big Tech and AI company leaders debating the merits of open-weight models, proprietary labs, and the practice of distillation—something that some Chinese labs have reportedly done to build models that compete with the latest efforts from Anthropic and others. On July 24, several companies including Nvidia, Hugging Face, Meta, Mistral, Mozilla, OpenAI and others co-signed an open letter titled “ Open Weights and American AI Leadership ” that argued for the value of open-weight models as opposed—or at least in addition—to proprietary ones, and defended distillation as a legitimate practice, even as it carved out a distinction for “unlawful efforts to extract value from closed models.” It advocated for “targeted legal and commercial frameworks rather than sweeping restrictions on techniques that play an important role in AI innovation.” On the topic of distillation, Meta and Zuckerberg wrote: The ability for models to learn from other models is an important principle of how the open source ecosystem works. All AI models are derived from human knowledge. Some have tried to frame distillation as harmful, but I think it is important to protect the principle that you can learn from anything you can observe. However, the majority of the essay focused on the notion of decentralized AI systems that are distributed widely. It also directly aims at arguments that tightly controlled, proprietary AI models and systems are needed because of concerns about existential threats or catastrophic misalignment: It is surprising that the discourse from many developing AI is so filled with doom. I do not understand why anyone who believes that AI will eliminate most jobs and much of humanity’s relevance would rush to build that future. The notion that AI is so dangerous that the only safe path is an extreme concentration of power seems inherently problematic. Zuckerberg wrote that the view of alignment taken by companies like Anthropic—which claims that it is trying to train singular and broad foundation models with operational parameters and guardrails that will ensure they benefit humanity broadly—is “fundamentally flawed.” “People’s diverse values represent different tradeoffs they would make on important issues. There is no technological solution that can align with everyone’s opposing interests and values at once,” the essay says. “Any singular superintelligence would have to prioritize some values over others and in the process would be incapable of being benevolent to everyone.” Instead, Meta argues here that models should be personalized to the needs and values of individuals or groups of individuals. It also claims that decentralization will make everyone safer, because it will give the benefits and advantages of “superintelligence” to everyone equally, instead of privileging “a small number of individuals, businesses, governments, or AI itself.” Meta has been lagging behind other big tech companies and major frontier labs for foundation models. Its models haven’t seen the kind of adoption that those developed by OpenAI or Anthropic have. OpenAI and Anthropic have aggressively targeted enterprise customers, releasing powerful models and harnesses for knowledge work tasks like software development, and they have made significant inroads and generated substantial revenue from this strategy. Meta has not seen the same level of success. Meta also saw a total overhaul of its AI division last year, when former Meta AI chief scientist Yann LeCun was replaced by former Scale AI CEO Alexandr Wang. The reset led to a change in focus.

凭借新的开放模式，Meta再次重启了其苦苦挣扎的人工智能战略
Meta宣布有意专注于开放权重大型语言模型。此外，该公司宣布发布一款名为Muse Glimmer的开放式模型，并承诺在未来几周内为其功能更强大的模型Muse Spark 1.2开放权重。除了这些公告，Meta首席执行官马克·扎克伯格还发表了一篇6000多字的文章，概述了该公司关于人工智能系统和治理的理念。这篇文章旨在将Meta与OpenAI和Anthropic等公司区分开来，OpenAI和Anthropic开发了专有模型，并游说美国政府帮助其与大规模蒸馏（涉及使用现有模型训练新模型）或中国实验室的开放重量模型竞争。Muse Glimmer是一个300亿参数的模型，默认情况下有128000个令牌上下文窗口。它是从Meta今年早些时候推出的更大、更强大的型号Muse Spark中提炼出来的。Glimmer是在用户的本地机器上运行的，而不是通过云服务或API。Glimmer的权重在Apache 2.0许可证下是开源的。Muse Spark于4月推出，是一个封闭的、专有的、前沿级的模型——这是Meta在去年对公司的人工智能团队进行重大改组后发布的第一个主要模型，与它对开放模型的关注有所不同。当Meta在7月发布Muse Spark 1.1时，它推出了第一个付费服务，这再次背离了之前的战略。Muse Spark 1.2于8月5日发布，并附带了终端编码代理Muse Code。开发人员普遍发现，Muse Code在性能上与Anthropic或OpenAI的前沿模型不太匹配，但它在成本上具有很好的竞争力，这意味着它与中国实验室的许多开放权重模型具有相似的定位。作为一款设计用于在消费级GPU上运行的小型模型，Muse Glimmer根本不会在这个级别上竞争，但它反映了一种日益增长的运动，即为本地设备带来一些推理，以减少对Anthropic和OpenAI等大型实验室生产的模型的依赖和支出。除了模型发布外，马克·扎克伯格还被认为是一封长篇公开信的作者，该信详细介绍了Meta在人工智能方面的企业战略，以及关于如何开发、分发和监管人工智能的更广泛论点。此前，其他大型科技公司和人工智能公司的领导人发表了几份声明，就开放式重量模型、专有实验室和蒸馏实践的优点进行了辩论——据报道，一些中国实验室已经这样做，以构建与Anthropic和其他公司的最新努力相竞争的模型。7月24日，包括Nvidia、Hugging Face、Meta、Mistral、Mozilla、OpenAI和其他公司在内的几家公司共同签署了一封题为“开放权重和美国人工智能领导力”的公开信，该信主张开放权重模型的价值，与专有模型相反，或者至少是除专有模型之外的价值，并为蒸馏作为一种合法做法辩护，尽管它对“从封闭模型中提取价值的非法行为”进行了区分。它主张“有针对性的法律和商业框架，而不是对在人工智能创新中发挥重要作用的技术进行全面限制。”关于蒸馏的话题，Meta和扎克伯格写道：模型从其他模型中学习的能力是开源生态系统运作的一个重要原则。所有的AI模型都源于人类知识。有些人试图将蒸馏视为有害的，但我认为重要的是要保护这样一个原则，即你可以从任何你能观察到的东西中学习。然而，本文的大部分内容都集中在广泛分布的去中心化人工智能系统的概念上。它还直接针对这样的论点，即由于对存在威胁或灾难性错位的担忧，需要严格控制的专有人工智能模型和系统：令人惊讶的是，许多正在开发的人工智能的话语充满了厄运。我不明白为什么那些认为人工智能将消除大多数工作岗位和人类大部分相关性的人会急于建立这样的未来。人工智能是如此危险，以至于唯一安全的途径是权力的极端集中，这种观点似乎天生就有问题。扎克伯格写道，像Anthropic这样的公司所采取的一致性观点是“根本有缺陷的”，该公司声称，它正试图用操作参数和护栏来训练单一而广泛的基础模型，以确保它们广泛造福人类。“人们的不同价值观代表了他们在重要问题上做出的不同权衡。没有技术解决方案可以同时与每个人的相反利益和价值观保持一致，”这篇文章说。“任何单一的超级智能都必须将某些价值观置于其他价值观之上，在此过程中无法对每个人都仁慈。”相反，Meta在这里认为，模型应该根据个人或群体的需求和价值观进行个性化。它还声称，去中心化将使每个人都更安全，因为它将把“超级智能”的好处和优势平等地给予每个人，而不是“少数个人、企业、政府或人工智能本身”。Meta在基础模型方面一直落后于其他大科技公司和主要前沿实验室。它的模型还没有像OpenAI或Anthropic开发的模型那样被采用。OpenAI和Anthropic积极瞄准企业客户，为软件开发等知识工作任务发布了强大的模型和工具，他们取得了重大进展，并从这一战略中获得了可观的收入。Meta还没有看到同样的成功。去年，Meta对其人工智能部门进行了全面改革，前Meta人工智能首席科学家Yann LeCun被前Scale人工智能首席执行官Alexandr Wang取代。重置导致焦点发生变化。
