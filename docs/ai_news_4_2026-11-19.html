Amidst fears of AI cyberattacks, Satya Nadella says lots of critical infrastructure is ‘unpatchable’
During his appearance at The New York Times’ DealBook Summit on Wednesday, Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella lamented the fact that a lot of America’s software systems — including those governing everyday hardware and transportation systems — suffer from unfixable software vulnerabilities that, in the age of AI-aided cyberattacks, are just waiting to be exploited. “Remember, there is a lot of stuff in our critical infrastructure that’s unpatchable,” Nadella said. “I mean, think about all the firmware and the chillers [a cooling device often used in data centers] and the HVAC systems everywhere else. So all of that, by the way, they’re on the internet.” In other words: The smartification of the economy, in which much of the world’s “dumb” devices were hooked up to the internet, is now causing tremendous headaches. The conversation took place against the backdrop of a wave of recent large-scale outages — including one involving Microsoft Outlook. Others have involved the NSA, the FFA, and Apple. Concerns have swirled that these outages are the result of cyberattacks, although officials have denied those claims. Still, the reality of AI-aided cyberattacks is spurring companies to find new strategies for vulnerability discovery and remediation. Nadella touched briefly on the recent Outlook situation, noting that the global inconvenience was the result of a “latent bug” that led to a “perfect storm.” “We put out an incident response, and we’re going to obviously take all of the learnings, both upstream architectural, as well as operational, and fix that,” he added. “Somebody was telling me about sort of even the transportation systems and the amount of stuff that’s discoverable but with unpatchable firmware,” Nadella also added on the topic of vulnerable systems. “So we’ll have to really modernize for sure … and then we will have to assess what the threat vector looks like, what our own ability to both detect and then defend in real time looks like.” Nadella made a broader point as well, which is that the process of patching software vulnerabilities has escalated to match the speed of potential exploitation, but that this has caused more problems. “We now have a very powerful tool that finds vulnerabilities,” he said. However, companies used to have more time to test those patches, understand a situation, and deploy them, he said. At the same time, AI has made sophisticated cyberattacks much easier to enact. “The patch [now] needs to go instantly from being available to being patched in production, because it’s being exploited in the wild, right? 周三，微软首席执行官萨蒂亚·纳德拉（Satya Nadella）在出席《纽约时报》交易簿峰会时哀叹，美国的许多软件系统，包括管理日常硬件和运输系统的软件系统，都存在无法修复的软件漏洞，在人工智能辅助网络攻击的时代，这些漏洞正等待被利用。纳德拉说：“记住，我们的关键基础设施中有很多东西是无法比拟的。”。“我的意思是，想想所有的固件和冷却器（数据中心经常使用的冷却设备）以及其他地方的暖通空调系统。所以，顺便说一句，所有这些都在互联网上。”换句话说：经济的智能化，世界上许多“愚蠢”的设备都连接到了互联网，现在正引起巨大的麻烦。这次对话是在最近一波大规模停电的背景下进行的，其中包括一起涉及微软Outlook的停电事件。其他人则涉及美国国家安全局、FFA和苹果公司。人们担心这些中断是网络攻击的结果，尽管官员们否认了这些说法。尽管如此，人工智能辅助网络攻击的现实正在促使公司寻找新的漏洞发现和修复策略。纳德拉简要谈到了最近的《展望》形势，指出全球不便是导致“完美风暴”的“潜在错误”造成的。他补充道：“我们发布了事件响应，我们显然会吸取所有经验教训，包括上游架构和运营方面的经验教训，并加以解决。”。纳德拉在谈到易受攻击的系统时补充道：“有人告诉我，甚至还有交通系统，以及可发现但固件不可修补的东西的数量。”。“所以我们必须真正实现现代化……然后我们必须评估威胁向量是什么样子的，我们自己实时检测和防御的能力是什么样子。”纳德拉也提出了一个更广泛的观点，即修补软件漏洞的过程已经升级，以匹配潜在的利用速度，但这造成了更多的问题。他说：“我们现在有一个非常强大的工具来发现漏洞。”。然而，他说，公司过去有更多的时间来测试这些补丁，了解情况并部署它们。与此同时，人工智能使复杂的网络攻击更容易实施。“补丁（现在）需要立即从可用状态进入生产状态，因为它在野外被利用了，对吧？所以这会造成不稳定。”当你通过我们文章中的链接购买时，我们可能会获得一小笔佣金。这并不影响我们的编辑独立性。
