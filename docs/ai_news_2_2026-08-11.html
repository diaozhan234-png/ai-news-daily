Mark Zuckerberg doesn’t understand how to live
An AI future of sleek, streamlined, and totally empty relationships. An AI future of sleek, streamlined, and totally empty relationships. Recently, a man I was rock climbing with told me about how he’d used AI to make a motivational poster for himself, which he’d hung on his bedroom wall: a bear, walking a slackline over a canyon, holding a sign that said, “Do cool shit.” I made what I hoped was a polite noise. What reaction was this man looking for? He probably hoped someone, maybe me, would say it was cool or inspiring. It seemed I was not the first person he’d told about this poster. “You know, it’s funny,” he said to me. “No one ever says anything about it.” I have been thinking about this now for several days. There’s a sort of turducken of things that I find off-putting all rolled into this anecdote: hippie shit, motivational posters, AI “art.” Still, the sum total of reactions to this poster appears to be polite noises. But what is the correct answer to someone showing you their slop? No one made it. Even bad art — for instance, a version of that motivational poster drawn by a person — requires effort on the part of the artist, and expresses the artist’s mind, both in the content and in the motor skills required to draw lines on a page. It requires a person’s time and attention. AI “art” requires typing a prompt into a box, and maybe refining the results a couple times. There’s nothing to react to. Zuckerberg is trying to be the soothing tech guru I bring this up because Mark Zuckerberg has posted a manifesto that he — or his communications team, or possibly an AI — wrote. The point of this 6,500-word essay is to define a positive future for AI. It strikes me as an inauspicious sign for AI that a new, supposedly world-changing and value-creating technology should need such a defense, but AI has been getting bad press these days. People hate data centers, and the higher electricity bills that come with them . Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei has said the technology may cause “unusually painful” job losses across multiple industries. OpenAI CEO Sam Altman’s house was the target of, first, a Molotov cocktail, and then gunfire . “It is surprising that the discourse from many developing AI is so filled with doom,” the essay says. “I do not understand why anyone who believes that AI will eliminate most jobs and much of humanity’s relevance would rush to build that future.” I find it difficult to believe that Zuckerberg doesn’t have Amodei’s phone number. Or Altman’s. I know he has Elon Musk’s — I’ve seen their texts in court. Perhaps he should have texted them to ask! Anyway, Zuckerberg is trying to be the soothing tech guru, here to tell us that things are going to be okay. He’s addressing, however belatedly, the rage that many people feel about AI. There are sections on data centers (Meta is building power sources for some data centers in the future, but don’t ask for renewable energy ) and “preventing government tyranny.” This essay exists to try to quiet the AI backlash — even if it never quite says that directly. Love is not just a feeling; it is a way of paying attention We will leave aside the question of “superintelligence,” which is Zuckerberg’s preferred term for what the rest of the industry calls “artificial general intelligence”; let’s stipulate, for the purposes of this essay, that it is indeed possible to create. I am also going to ignore the risible defense of the pervert glasses contained in this piece; they are indefensible. As for the risks, I think they are best addressed elsewhere. What I am interested in is what Zuckerberg — or whoever is writing for him — believes will improve all our lives. Here is his first pitch: “Everyone will have an exceptionally capable personal agent that understands you, your goals, and everything you care about. Your agent will work 24/7 on your behalf to improve your relationships, health, career, finances, home management, hobbies, and more.” I am struck, here, by the inclusion of “relationships” and “hobbies.” What strengthens a relationship? Almost always, it is personal investment. Perhaps an AI agent might be better at predicting what my father will want for his birthday than I am, but it definitionally cannot give him my time and consideration. Love is not just a feeling; it is a way of paying attention. Zuckerberg’s vision of maximizing productivity comes at the cost of my experience

一个光滑、流线型、完全空洞的关系的人工智能未来。一个光滑、流线型、完全空洞的关系的人工智能未来。最近，一个和我一起攀岩的人告诉我，他是如何用人工智能为自己制作一张励志海报的，他把海报挂在卧室的墙上：一只熊，在峡谷上走着一条懒绳，举着一块写着“做酷狗屎”的牌子。我希望这是一个礼貌的声音。这个人想要什么样的反应？他可能希望有人，也许是我，会说这很酷或鼓舞人心。似乎我不是他告诉这张海报的第一个人。“你知道吗，这很有趣，”他对我说。“从来没有人说过这件事。”我已经想了好几天了。我发现这件轶事中有一种令人反感的东西：嬉皮士狗屎、励志海报、人工智能“艺术”。尽管如此，人们对这张海报的反应似乎都是礼貌的声音。但是，当有人向你展示他们的垃圾时，正确的答案是什么？没有人能做到。即使是糟糕的艺术——例如，一个人画的励志海报的一个版本——也需要艺术家的努力，并在内容和在页面上画线所需的运动技能上表达艺术家的想法。这需要一个人的时间和注意力。人工智能“艺术”需要在框中键入提示，并可能对结果进行几次改进。没有什么可反应的。扎克伯格试图成为我提到的舒缓的技术大师，因为马克·扎克伯格发布了一份他或他的通信团队，或者可能是人工智能写的宣言。这篇6500字的文章的重点是为人工智能定义一个积极的未来。在我看来，这对人工智能来说是一个不吉利的信号，一种新的、本应改变世界和创造价值的技术应该需要这样的防御，但人工智能最近一直受到负面报道。人们讨厌数据中心，以及随之而来的更高的电费。Anthropic首席执行官Dario Amodei表示，这项技术可能会在多个行业造成“异常痛苦”的失业。OpenAI首席执行官Sam Altman的房子首先是燃烧弹的目标，然后是枪声。文章称：“令人惊讶的是，许多正在发展的人工智能的话语中充满了厄运。”。“我不明白为什么那些认为人工智能将消除大多数工作岗位和人类大部分相关性的人会急于建立这样的未来。”我很难相信扎克伯格没有阿莫迪的电话号码。或者奥特曼的。我知道他有埃隆·马斯克的——我在法庭上看过他们的短信。也许他应该给他们发短信问问！不管怎样，扎克伯格试图成为一位舒缓的科技大师，在这里告诉我们一切都会好起来的。他正在解决许多人对人工智能的愤怒，尽管为时已晚。有关于数据中心（Meta正在为未来的一些数据中心建造电源，但不要要求可再生能源）和“防止政府暴政”的部分。这篇文章的存在是为了平息人工智能的反弹——即使它从来没有直接这么说。爱不仅仅是一种感觉；这是一种关注的方式我们将把“超级智能”的问题放在一边，这是扎克伯格对行业其他人所说的“通用人工智能”的首选术语；为了本文的目的，让我们规定，创造确实是可能的。我也将忽略这篇文章中对变态眼镜的可笑辩护；他们是站不住脚的。至于风险，我认为最好在其他地方解决。我感兴趣的是扎克伯格——或者为他写作的人——相信什么会改善我们的生活。这是他的第一个建议：“每个人都会有一个非常有能力的私人代理人，他了解你、你的目标以及你关心的一切。你的代理人将全天候为你工作，以改善你的关系、健康、职业、财务、家庭管理、爱好等等。”在这里，我对“关系”和“爱好”的加入感到震惊。是什么加强了一段关系？几乎总是个人投资。也许人工智能代理可能比我更擅长预测我父亲生日想要什么，但从定义上讲，它不能给他我的时间和考虑。爱不仅仅是一种感觉；这是一种专注的方式。扎克伯格最大限度地提高生产力的愿景是以我的经验为代价的
